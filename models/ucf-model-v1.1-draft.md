# UCF model v1.1: reconciliation draft

**Date:** 2026-09-16  
**Status:** proposed conceptual revision; AI-assisted; author review pending; not empirically validated.  
**Predecessor:** [model v1](ucf-model-v1.md), preserved unchanged.

## 1. Definition and assessment boundary

**Proposed working definition:** Functional coherence is the context-dependent fit among a system's assumptions, behavior, dependencies, stated purposes, and the conditions in which it operates.

This definition reconciles v1's internal/external/temporal fit, the earlier glossary's integration language, and Root Sequence's model/reality framing. It is a vocabulary proposal, not an operational measure until an application specifies observations and decision rules.

Keep three assessment questions distinct:

1. **Functional fit:** Does behavior match the stated purpose and actual operating conditions?
2. **Relational consequences:** What happens across the chosen boundary, including displaced work, resource demands, uncertainty, and feedback?
3. **Normative judgment:** Whose purposes and decision rights are accepted or contested; who bears costs; what objections remain?

These are related questions, not independent quantities or three scores to aggregate. Evidence of consistency cannot alone authorize a purpose. Normative commitments must not be disguised as physical facts. A boundary that excludes affected dependencies can make a narrow description accurate yet insufficient.

## 2. Four analytical layers

| Layer | Retained from v1 | Required questions |
|---|---|---|
| Internal | Processes, models, state integrity | What assumptions and targets are checked? What is uncertain or contradicted? |
| Inter-agent | Communication and coordination | What must cross an interface? What can remain private or different? |
| Systemic | Larger dependencies and structures | Where do burdens go? Which constraints or excluded parties alter the conclusion? |
| Temporal | Continuity, adaptation, revision | What changes over the chosen horizon? What needs maintenance? What cannot be undone? |

No layer is automatically the correct scale, and they need not agree. Describe omissions and stop expanding the boundary when the remaining exclusions can be stated and justified for the question. Reopen it when a material dependency changes.

## 3. Four optional state labels

These labels come from UCF's overview and glossary; they are not the four layers above.

- **Chaos:** shorthand for unstable or difficult-to-coordinate conditions in a specified observation window, not automatically mathematical chaos.
- **Tension:** unresolved constraints or conflicting signals; it can carry information rather than mark a defect.
- **Flow:** adaptive functioning relative to a task and conditions, not maximal efficiency or permanent stability.
- **Unity:** proposed coordination across differentiated parts, not identical beliefs, complete integration, shared consciousness, or an endpoint.

Attach a label only after describing the observations. Permit multiple labels, uncertainty, or no label. No ordinal scale, universal transition sequence, or mandatory progression is assumed. The [booking example](examples/booking-service/README.md) tests whether these labels add explanatory detail; its first editorial pass does not establish that they do.

Alternatives remain open: retain the names with local definitions; replace them with domain-specific descriptors; or omit the state taxonomy while keeping useful layered questions. No alternative is adopted across the project by this draft alone.

## 4. Dynamics and real constraints

Retain v1's interest in feedback, stressors, integrity, and adaptation, but examine each proposed mechanism. Amplification may intensify error as well as learning; integration may create harmful coupling; separation may contain failure. Do not define every successful change as integration or every disruption as loss.

Conditions may make some behavior easier to sustain without guaranteeing it. Changes in permissions, resources, or information need a causal account and alternatives. Capability, authority, outcomes, and moral status remain separate.

A transition may require preserving a service while ending an old arrangement. Track intermediate dependencies, maintenance, refusal, shortages, outages, wrong predictions, and simultaneous failures. Money or compute becoming less constraining does not establish that energy, materials, time, ecology, or agreement cease to matter.

## 5. Direction without a final state

Compare named changes over specified periods. Gains may reverse or conflict across relationships; some comparisons remain unresolved. The framework neither proves a perfectly coherent world impossible nor presumes one achievable. It requires no theorem about the universe's initial condition to assess a bounded repair.

Imagination and scenarios can expose assumptions and propose alternatives. They do not supply evidence that those alternatives will work. More accurate prediction is not the only goal; retaining the ability to notice, respond, and revise is a proposed design concern to test.

## 6. Indicators, not a universal score

Select task-specific observations before interpreting results. Candidates might include error propagation, unmet demand, recovery time, maintenance burden, interface failures, or documented unresolved objections. Each needs its own definition and data limits; qualitative evidence may be appropriate.

Do not reduce dissent, privacy, personhood, experience, justice, or flourishing to a coherence number. Optimization remains possible inside stated constraints, with explicit choices about objectives and authority. Avoid inventing a scalar merely to make comparison easy.

## 7. What would count against UCF?

The layer review should be revised or abandoned for an application if it adds no useful distinctions beyond an ordinary competent assessment, requires excessive data or effort, or obscures consequential disagreement. The state labels should be narrowed or dropped when they cannot be distinguished except after the outcome is known.

Cross-domain claims need a defined mapping, evidence of what is shared, and cases where transfer fails. Similar wording is not a shared causal mechanism. Formal models and empirical studies have different evidential roles.

The [claims register](../docs/claims.md) tracks these commitments. The [evidence rules](../docs/evidence.md) distinguish conceptual proposals, mathematical demonstrations, and empirical support. A toy model illustrates consequences of chosen assumptions; it does not validate this framework.

The Root Sequence paper can cite this candidate as internal project provenance while testing its definitions against other methods. UCF owns its specific model; the paper owns its broader synthesis.
