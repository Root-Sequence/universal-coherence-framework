# Dynamic coherence evidence reconciliation — 2026-09-24

**Status:** AI-assisted evidence-mapping pass for author review  
**Purpose:** Compare the new Root Sequence dynamic-coherence/adaptive-continuity synthesis against established adjacent literatures before UCF adopts, renames, narrows, or rejects any part of it.  
**Scope:** Targeted literature check, not a systematic review, meta-analysis, or validation of UCF.  
**Source framework:** [Root Sequence draft PR #21](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/root-sequence/pull/21)

## Bottom line

The September 24 synthesis appears to fill a real **integration gap** in UCF, but it does **not** establish a new universal scientific theory.

Several parts have strong or serious neighbors in existing work:

- adaptation and viability;
- path dependence;
- dynamical systems and attractor-like behavior;
- ecological resilience, adaptability, and transformability;
- allostasis and cumulative stress load;
- embodied/enactive accounts of agency;
- capability as real rather than merely formal opportunity;
- controllability, reachability, and information-theoretic empowerment.

The useful opportunity is therefore not to claim discovery of all of these mechanisms at once. It is to build a disciplined **translation layer** that asks how they differ, where they overlap, and when UCF's own vocabulary adds value.

The audit also strengthens a concern already recorded in UCF issue #1: several older Foundation and Speculation pages state cross-domain claims much more strongly than the current evidence convention permits.

---

## Method

### Questions

For each candidate concept, ask:

1. Is there an established or serious adjacent literature?
2. What does that literature actually claim within its own domain?
3. What part of the Root Sequence synthesis does it support, constrain, or complicate?
4. What does it **not** justify?
5. What comparison would be needed before UCF can claim added value?

### Source-selection rule for this pass

Prefer:

- original or primary research when readily accessible;
- established reviews or reference works when the concept spans a literature;
- exact papers already named in the Root Sequence draft;
- sources that create constraints or counterexamples, not only supportive analogies.

### Limits

This pass did **not**:

- perform a systematic database search;
- preregister inclusion criteria;
- assess publication bias;
- reproduce experiments;
- calculate effect sizes;
- review all citations within each source;
- establish novelty for "dynamic coherence";
- establish that one causal mechanism spans the compared domains.

The result is a **related-work and evidence-boundary map**.

## 1. Adaptivity, viability, and agency

Ezequiel Di Paolo's *Autopoiesis, Adaptivity, Teleology, Agency* develops an enactive account in which adaptivity involves a system's regulation relative to conditions of viability. That is directly relevant to the intuition that not every state change is an adaptation and that some changes alter future regulation.

**Permitted use:**

- evidence that a serious theoretical tradition links living autonomy, viability, adaptivity, and agency;
- a source of distinctions to compare against UCF.

**Not permitted:**

- "Di Paolo proves the UCF transition equation";
- "all adaptive systems have agency in the same sense";
- "adaptivity implies consciousness";
- direct transfer from organismic viability to institutions or machine agents without an explicit mapping.

Source: Ezequiel A. Di Paolo, "Autopoiesis, Adaptivity, Teleology, Agency," *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 4 (2005), 429–452.  
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11097-005-9002-y

## 2. Allostasis is a bounded biological anchor

Bruce McEwen's work is particularly useful for one narrow proposition: regulatory responses can support adaptation in the short run while cumulative or poorly regulated activation can create longer-run costs.

That supports the **shape** of the Root Sequence example:

    condition
        ↓
    regulatory response
        ↓
    short-run benefit
        ↓
    repeated / persistent activation
        ↓
    longer-run cost

It does not license calling every persistent institutional, social, or artificial behavior "allostatic."

Sources:

- Bruce S. McEwen, "Stress, Adaptation, and Disease: Allostasis and Allostatic Load," *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 840 (1998), 33–44.  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9629234/
- Bruce S. McEwen, "Physiology and Neurobiology of Stress and Adaptation: Central Role of the Brain," *Physiological Reviews* 87 (2007), 873–904.  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17615391/

Human trauma remains a distinct clinical and experiential topic. SAMHSA's event–experience–effects framing is useful precisely because it resists reducing trauma to stress physiology alone.

## 3. Resilience needs separation from adaptability and transformation

Holling's classic ecological paper distinguishes resilience from stability. Walker, Holling, Carpenter, and Kinzig later distinguish **resilience**, **adaptability**, and **transformability** in social-ecological systems.

This directly challenges older UCF language such as:

> "Resilience determines how long coherence lasts."

and ordinal statements such as:

> "Chaos: brittle. Tension: unstable. Flow: durable. Unity: powerful ..."

Those may be speculative story-grammar, but they are not established resilience results.

A stronger UCF treatment should ask:

- Which identity or regime is being preserved?
- What disturbance is considered?
- Does the system recover within the same regime?
- Do actors deliberately adapt the regime?
- Is transformation to a different regime the relevant outcome?

Sources:

- C. S. Holling, "Resilience and Stability of Ecological Systems," *Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics* 4 (1973), 1–23.  
  https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.04.110173.000245
- Brian Walker, C. S. Holling, Stephen R. Carpenter, and Ann Kinzig, "Resilience, Adaptability and Transformability in Social–ecological Systems," *Ecology and Society* 9(2):5 (2004).  
  https://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol9/iss2/art5/

## 4. Metastability is valuable precisely because it is specific

Tognoli and Kelso use metastability in coordination dynamics and neuroscience to characterize systems in which tendencies toward integration coexist with tendencies toward autonomous activity.

This is genuinely relevant to Root Sequence's intuition:

> stable enough to coordinate, flexible enough to reorganize.

But that sentence is **an analogy**, not the technical definition.

Do not write:

- "healthy societies are metastable";
- "identity is metastability";
- "coherence requires metastability";

without a domain model that earns the term.

Source: Emmanuelle Tognoli and J. A. Scott Kelso, "The Metastable Brain," *Neuron* 81 (2014), 35–48.  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3997258/

## 5. Attractors can model personality, but the word must stay expensive

Shoda, LeeTiernan, and Mischel explicitly model personality as a dynamical system in which stable attractor states can emerge from cognitive-affective interactions. Nowak and colleagues similarly develop formal/computational attractor accounts of personality stability and change.

That means Root Sequence was right **not** to ban attractor language entirely.

The evidence-boundary is:

> If a domain actually defines state variables and transition dynamics, "attractor" may be literal or model-relative. Otherwise, "attractor-like pattern" is a structural analogy.

Sources:

- Yuichi Shoda, Scott LeeTiernan, and Walter Mischel, "Personality as a Dynamical System: Emergence of Stability and Distinctiveness from Intra- and Interpersonal Interactions," *Personality and Social Psychology Review* 6(4) (2002), 316–325.  
  https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327957PSPR0604_06
- Andrzej Nowak, Robin R. Vallacher, and Michal Zochowski, "The emergence of personality: Dynamic foundations of individual variation," *Developmental Review* 25 (2005), 351–385.  
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dr.2005.10.004

## 6. "Agency envelope" should be compared against capabilities, reachability, and empowerment

The proposed agency-envelope concept currently mixes at least five distinctions:

1. what the world physically/institutionally permits;
2. what the actor knows exists;
3. what the actor perceives as feasible;
4. what the actor is authorized to do;
5. what resources/support make practically executable.

That is broader than any one checked comparator.

### Capability approach

The capability approach distinguishes **real or substantive opportunities** from options that exist only formally or legally.

This is a close conceptual neighbor for the claim that nominal choice count is not enough.

Reference overview: Ingrid Robeyns and Morten Fibieger Byskov, "The Capability Approach," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*.  
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/

### Viability theory

Viability theory studies constrained dynamical systems and the sets of states from which viable evolution is possible under available controls.

This gives a formal comparator for constrained reachability.

Reference: Jean-Pierre Aubin, *Viability Theory* and related viability-kernel work.  
https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-0-8176-4910-4

### Information-theoretic empowerment

Klyubin, Polani, and Nehaniv introduced "empowerment" as the information-theoretic capacity of an agent's action channel; later work connects it to controllability and observability in continuous agent-environment systems.

This formalizes **potential causal influence**, not social/political empowerment or legitimate authority.

Sources:

- Alexander S. Klyubin, Daniel Polani, and Chrystopher L. Nehaniv, "Empowerment: a universal agent-centric measure of control," IEEE Congress on Evolutionary Computation (2005), 128–135.  
  https://doi.org/10.1109/CEC.2005.1554676
- Tobias Jung, Daniel Polani, and Peter Stone, "Empowerment for continuous agent–environment systems," *Adaptive Behavior* 19(1) (2011).  
  https://doi.org/10.1177/1059712310392389

### Working conclusion

Do **not** claim that "agency envelope" is a new measurement.

Instead, treat it as a project-level routing concept that says:

> For a specific actor, map the relationship among reachable, known, perceived-feasible, authorized, and materially supported actions.

Then compare whether an established framework can do the job more precisely.

## 7. Dynamic coherence: what may actually be new

The likely contribution is **not** any individual component.

It may be the insistence that a coherence assessment simultaneously keep visible:

- present functional fit;
- transition dynamics;
- path dependence;
- feedback quality;
- power over revision;
- future reachable possibilities;
- continuity criteria;
- externalized costs;
- scale and timescale;
- the possibility that maintaining coherence requires transformation.

That is a candidate synthesis.

Novelty has **not** been established.

A serious next step is to compare it against:

- ordinary systems engineering / resilience review;
- viability and reachability analysis where formal state models are possible;
- socio-technical hazard / safety methods;
- capability-oriented analysis for human agency;
- resilience/adaptability/transformability frameworks;
- enactive accounts of adaptivity for living systems.

If those methods already recover the same consequential distinctions with less conceptual overhead, UCF should borrow or route rather than rename.

## 7A. Cybernetic and organizational-learning predecessors

A further prior-art pass materially narrows the novelty space.

### Ashby: variable versus parameter change

W. Ross Ashby explicitly distinguished change in a variable/state from change in a parameter/field, using the distinction to separate changes that are behavior from changes from one behavior regime to another.

This is a direct predecessor of the Root Sequence state-versus-transition-dynamics distinction.

Source:

- Ashby, "Feedback, Adaptation and Stability" (1960): https://panarchy.org/ashby/adaptation.1960.html

### Ashby: ultrastability

Ashby's ultrastable system changes parameters when ordinary dynamics drive essential variables toward critical states.

This is a strong predecessor for second-order adaptation, not a general theory of institutions, agency, or consciousness.

### Requisite variety

Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety formally constrains regulation by the regulator's available variety/information capacity relative to disturbances.

This is relevant to response diversity but is not a measure of human agency or legitimate power.

### Good Regulator theorem

Conant and Ashby's Good Regulator theorem, under its formal assumptions, makes model formation central to effective regulation.

This is relevant to UCF's model/feedback emphasis but does not authorize one regulator or convert prediction into legitimacy.

### Double-loop learning

Argyris and Schön's organizational-learning distinction between changing action strategies and changing governing variables is a close organizational predecessor to rule/transition revision.

For organizational cases, UCF should route to this established literature rather than redescribe it as a new coherence mechanism.

### Resilience engineering and sustained adaptability

Hollnagel's respond/monitor/learn/anticipate potentials and Woods's graceful extensibility / sustained adaptability directly overlap UCF concerns about preserving adaptive capacity under changing conditions.

In particular, Woods's sustained adaptability is close to the proposed idea of preserving capacity for future adaptation.

### Implication

The state/rule distinction, adaptive regulation, response diversity, model-based regulation, and sustained adaptability should **not** be presented as new UCF discoveries.

If UCF contributes anything, it must be in the disciplined synthesis and cross-domain routing—not ownership of those mechanisms.

Root Sequence's lineage note is the detailed project provenance:

https://github.com/Root-Sequence/root-sequence/blob/dynamic-coherence-adaptive-continuity-2026-09-24/research/dynamic-coherence-lineage.md

## 7B. Naming collision: "dynamic coherence" is prior terminology

The phrase **dynamic coherence** has prior uses that are materially different from the Root Sequence synthesis.

Examples identified in this pass include:

- Lane and Oliva (1998), using dynamic coherence in a synthesis of system dynamics and soft systems methodology to refer to consistency between intuitive behavior and behavior derived from causal structure;
- St-Louis (2026), "Dynamic Coherence Windows," a structural-coherence measurement framework for AI-system outputs.

Therefore UCF should not imply:

- coinage;
- terminological exclusivity;
- continuity with those frameworks without explicit comparison.

"Dynamic coherence" remains provisional project language pending naming review.

## 7C. Critical systems predecessors: power, boundary, legitimacy, and method pluralism

A further pass substantially narrows the novelty of UCF's boundary/power questions.

### Critical Systems Heuristics

Werner Ulrich's Critical Systems Heuristics (CSH) explicitly treats system boundaries as **boundary judgments** and organizes twelve critical questions around four sources of influence:

- motivation / purpose;
- control / power;
- knowledge;
- legitimacy.

CSH also distinguishes people who are **involved** in an intervention from those who are **affected** but lack comparable control.

This is a close predecessor to UCF questions about:

- whose purpose defines success;
- who controls resources and revision;
- whose knowledge counts;
- who bears externalized consequences;
- who is affected but excluded from authority;
- whether the assessment boundary hides relevant people or effects.

Sources:

- Werner Ulrich & Martin Reynolds, "Critical Systems Heuristics": https://wulrich.com/downloads/ulrich_reynolds_2010_prepub.pdf
- Werner Ulrich, CSH overview: https://wulrich.com/downloads/ulrich_2002b.pdf

### Critical Systems Thinking

Critical Systems Thinking (CST) explicitly rejects universal reliance on one systems method. Its traditions emphasize:

- critical awareness;
- social awareness;
- methodological / theoretical pluralism;
- human emancipation and attention to power.

Gerald Midgley's compressed formulation emphasizes critical awareness, emancipation, and methodological pluralism.

This strongly resembles the methodological correction produced by UCF's own editorial pilots:

> use competent domain methods first; use UCF only where its synthesis adds a consequential connection or omission.

Sources:

- Michael C. Jackson, "The origins and nature of critical systems thinking," *Systemic Practice and Action Research* 4 (1991), 131–149.
- Gerald Midgley, "What is this thing called CST?" (1996).

### Soft Systems Methodology

Checkland's Soft Systems Methodology (SSM) foregrounds multiple worldviews and problem framings. CATWOE makes explicit customers/affected parties, actors, transformation, worldview, ownership, and environmental constraints.

For contested human problem situations, SSM may therefore be a better-established method than asking UCF to rediscover that different actors define the "system" differently.

### Implication

UCF should **not** claim novelty for:

- boundary critique;
- affected-but-not-involved stakeholders;
- linking purpose to power and legitimacy;
- methodological pluralism;
- multiple-worldview problem framing.

The candidate UCF contribution becomes narrower:

> a lightweight routing/synthesis layer that helps users notice which established systems tradition or domain method is appropriate, while preserving cross-project vocabulary where that genuinely reduces fragmentation.

That proposition still requires testing.

## 7D. Organizational cybernetics predecessor: the Viable System Model

Stafford Beer's Viable System Model (VSM) links organizational viability with recursive organization, local autonomy, coordination, environmental scanning/adaptation, and organizational identity/policy.

This overlaps UCF/Root Sequence questions about:

- continuity while changing;
- local autonomy versus wider cohesion;
- sensing environmental change;
- future-facing adaptation;
- organizational identity.

VSM also makes a strong theoretical claim about organizational viability and has a substantial case/application literature plus later empirical tests.

UCF should therefore treat VSM as a serious comparator, not silently reinvent organizational viability through coherence terminology.

Relevant sources include:

- Espinosa, Harnden & Walker (2008), "A complexity approach to sustainability – Stafford Beer revisited," *European Journal of Operational Research*.
- Schwaninger & Scheef (2016), "A Test of the Viable System Model: Theoretical Claim vs. Empirical Evidence," *Cybernetics and Systems*.

The appropriate question is not whether UCF can redescribe VSM concepts.

It is whether UCF adds a useful cross-domain route **without reducing organizational cybernetics to generic coherence language**.

## 8. What should change in UCF now

### Keep

- functional fit plus relational consequences plus normative judgment;
- four analytical layers as prompts;
- anti-scalar and anti-conformity rules;
- explicit boundaries and timescales;
- refusal to infer consciousness, legitimacy, or moral worth from coherence;
- the booking example as a toy demonstration of bounded objective conflict.

### Extend

The **temporal layer** should explicitly ask:

- what changed only in current state;
- what changed in future transition behavior;
- what prior history is causally retained;
- which options became more or less reachable;
- what adaptation created new dependency or lock-in;
- what would cause the system to revise again.

### Reclassify or correct

Older pages that:

- call the four UCF labels generalized attractors across systems;
- describe Chaos → Tension → Flow → Unity as a real developmental trajectory;
- rank Flow or Unity as inherently more resilient;
- treat cross-scale coherence requirements as universal;
- describe "coherence" as a standard systems-theory quantity without a scoped source;
- imply biological trauma mechanisms generalize directly to artificial or institutional systems.

### Do not do yet

- rename UCF around "dynamic coherence";
- create a dynamic-coherence score;
- replace four state labels with another universal taxonomy;
- claim a grand theory of identity, trauma, intelligence, or consciousness;
- rewrite every historical document as though the September 24 synthesis had always been present.

## 9. Proposed comparative method

The next UCF validation should not ask:

> Can UCF describe this case?

Almost any flexible framework can describe a case after the fact.

Instead:

### Protocol

1. Select one bounded public case before analysis.
2. Define the decision question and evidence packet.
3. Choose at least one competent baseline method appropriate to the domain.
4. Freeze the UCF/dynamic-coherence prompt before reviewing outputs.
5. Give baseline and UCF reviewers the same evidence.
6. Record:
   - consequential omissions found;
   - false alarms;
   - time / burden;
   - additional information demanded;
   - disagreements;
   - which findings changed a plausible decision;
   - whether each finding was unique or already captured by baseline.
7. Include a null result if UCF adds nothing.
8. Publish the exact prompt, evidence packet, rubric, and disagreements.

### First case recommendation

Use a **non-clinical, non-sensitive infrastructure or service-design case** rather than trauma, consciousness, or intimate personal history.

Good candidates:

- service fallback / provider lock-in;
- transit accessibility under disruption;
- repairable device/service architecture;
- community infrastructure degraded mode.

These cases allow real dependencies, agency, maintenance, and path dependence without requiring speculative inference about inner experience.

## 10. Candidate review questions

A reviewer applying the dynamic extension should answer:

1. **Present fit:** What currently works, for whom, under what assumptions?
2. **Transition:** What changes immediately?
3. **History:** Which effects persist, and through what mechanism?
4. **Future possibility:** Which meaningful actions become easier, harder, or impossible?
5. **Feedback:** Which consequences will become visible, to whom, and when?
6. **Revision power:** Who can change the arrangement after mismatch appears?
7. **Continuity:** What capability, relationship, identity, or function is supposed to persist?
8. **Transformation:** Under what evidence would preserving the old configuration stop being the goal?
9. **Externalities:** Which costs or risks move outside the local boundary?
10. **Uncertainty:** What remains unknown or contested?
11. **Baseline comparison:** Which of these findings would a competent existing method already identify?
12. **Retirement test:** What result would make this vocabulary not worth using?

## 11A. Editorial method pilots: near-null result

After this evidence map was drafted, Root Sequence ran two AI-assisted editorial comparisons on the same public technical incident (Cloudflare, June 20, 2024).

The purpose was protocol debugging, not independent validation.

### Pilot 001

The evidence packet included the source postmortem's remediation section.

### Pilot 002

The same case and prompts were used, but the source-authored future-remediation section was intentionally withheld.

### Repeated result

In both pilots:

- the competent ordinary incident/system review captured nearly all decision-relevant findings generated by the frozen dynamic-coherence review;
- the dynamic review produced **zero** findings coded simultaneously as evidence-supported, decision-relevant, and substantively absent from the ordinary review;
- the ordinary review surfaced a simple component-level containment action—runtime execution bounds/watchdogs—that the dynamic review omitted in both pilots;
- Dynamic Coherence's strongest contribution was integrative framing around state versus transition change, path dependence, shrinking recovery options, continuity, and revision capacity.

Because the same AI-assisted process generated and coded all outputs, these counts are **not independent performance evidence**.

They do, however, justify a methodological correction:

> **Do not treat Dynamic Coherence as a general replacement for domain methods. Treat it as a candidate routing/synthesis layer whose added value must be demonstrated case by case.**

For technical incidents, competent reliability/safety/incident methods remain primary.

For human agency, capability-oriented methods remain primary.

For formal reachability, viability/control methods remain primary.

For trauma, clinical and trauma research remain primary.

For consciousness, consciousness research remains primary.

UCF should only claim added value when the cross-domain synthesis exposes a consequential omission or relationship that those methods do not already handle adequately.

## 12. Decision rule

The September 24 insight should change UCF **where it exposes a missing variable or invalid inference**.

It should not change UCF merely because the new language is elegant.

A useful test for every rewrite is:

> **What becomes more accurate, testable, discriminating, or actionable after this change?**

If the answer is only "the page sounds more unified," preserve the idea as interpretation rather than rewriting the model around it.
